## What Discovery is for

Start with one process, one accountable reviewer, and a decision that can be explained. Discovery should clarify the work, current systems, bottlenecks, evidence gaps, risks, and a sensible next step. A valid result can be **proceed**, **measure first**, **wait**, or **do not automate**.

The [public Discovery page](https://ziontechgroup.com/discovery/) describes a process map, order-of-magnitude ROI estimate, executive report, and a 30-minute review session. It advertises a seven-day diagnostic and a business-response target. Those are observed source statements, not independently tested service performance or commitments made by this repository. The [showcase](https://ziontechgroup.com/apps-network-showcase/) describes on-screen results and requested email copies, with delivery dependent on the provider and recipient inbox.

## Benefits and the evidence needed

| Benefit | Useful output | Evidence that makes it credible | What not to claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared process understanding | A bounded map of actors, systems, queues, and handoffs | Process-owner review and a documented scope | A complete enterprise transformation plan |
| Better prioritization | A written proceed/wait/do-not-automate recommendation | Explicit decision criteria and unresolved assumptions | That AI is automatically the best solution |
| More realistic economics | A scenario using baseline volume, time, cost, and adoption assumptions | Dated inputs, sensitivity ranges, and a measurement plan | Guaranteed savings, payback, or revenue |
| More focused app evaluation | A shortlist of relevant catalog entries and planning tools | Source links, maturity labels, and acceptance criteria | That a catalog listing is production-ready |
| Lower coordination friction | A client-safe summary and a clearly owned next step | Human review and separately tracked routing legs | Confirmed email delivery merely because a form was accepted |
| Earlier risk visibility | Privacy, security, integration, and data-quality questions | Named reviewers and explicit release gates | Certification, regulatory compliance, or security protection without evidence |
| A practical stopping rule | A documented reason to pause or decline implementation | Stakeholder agreement on the stop condition | That the questionnaire enrolls a client into paid work |

## A useful first-process brief

| Question | Good starting material |
| --- | --- |
| What work repeats? | A short description of one recurring workflow |
| Who owns it? | A role or accountable reviewer; use names only with an appropriate basis |
| Where does work stall? | Queue, handoff, rework, and exception examples |
| What is the baseline? | Measured volume, time, quality, or a proposed measurement window |
| Which systems are involved? | Tool names and integration constraints, not credentials |
| What is sensitive? | Data categories, access restrictions, and retention needs |
| What would count as success? | A measurable acceptance criterion and a stop condition |

Use fictional or sanitized examples first. Do not upload credentials, patient records, bank details, confidential contracts, or production exports merely to explore a questionnaire or calculator.

## ROI is a scenario, not an outcome

A useful estimate separates observed inputs from assumptions. For example: monthly eligible volume × baseline time per case × hourly cost × realistically addressable share gives a modeled labor-cost envelope. Adoption, review effort, ongoing costs, implementation costs, and uncertainty still need to be accounted for. Do not double-count time savings as both payroll reduction and new revenue. Compare the estimate with a measured baseline before reporting an achieved result.

The [FinOps scenario estimator](app-index.md#app-051) and [pilot guide](app-index.md#app-050) are planning resources. Their availability does not establish cloud-account access or achieved savings.

## What happens next

1. Capture the seven business answers and an email using the [questionnaire specification](commercial-questionnaire.md).
2. Validate scope, data permissions, and the intended recipients.
3. Generate and review a client-safe summary with assumptions and limitations.
4. Track the client and Commercial copies separately using the [routing workflow](commercial-routing.md).
5. Review next steps: stop, measure, clarify, separately scoped consulting, or an approved pilot.
6. Apply the [rollout gates](discovery-rollout.md) before any live implementation.

## Readiness checklist

- [ ] A process owner and reviewer are identified.
- [ ] Baseline inputs are measured or explicitly marked unknown.
- [ ] The free versus paid offer distinction is reconciled.
- [ ] The client-safe summary contains no internal-only information.
- [ ] Recipient validation, privacy notice, and retention rules are approved.
- [ ] Both delivery legs and their failure cases have been tested under separate authorization.
- [ ] No runtime, financial, compliance, or delivery guarantee exceeds the available evidence.
